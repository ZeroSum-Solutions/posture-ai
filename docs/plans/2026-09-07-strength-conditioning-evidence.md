# Adult strength and conditioning programming: evidence annex

> Implementation authority: [the approved PRD](2026-09-07-strength-conditioning-prd.md) and its normative fixtures. This annex preserves research and earlier candidates; conflicting RIR ranges, progression rules, or delivery sequencing are superseded by the PRD.

Date checked: 2026-09-07
Population: generally healthy adults. This is a product-design evidence map, not an individualized exercise prescription or medical-clearance protocol.

## Decision summary

Posture AI should use a simple, logged, autoregulated strength plan as the default. The stable core is at least two resistance-training exposures per week, major muscle groups, mostly two to three work sets, progressive increases in repetitions or load, and conditioning that builds toward WHO weekly activity guidance. Training to failure and complex periodization are optional rather than default requirements.

The product should treat 4-, 6-, 8-, and 12-week selections as planning and review horizons, not biologically proven cycle lengths. Eight weeks is a reasonable default because it is long enough to observe a trend and is common in trials, but the 2026 ACSM synthesis included interventions from 6 to 52 weeks and did not establish a best cycle duration. Four-week plans should be described as an onboarding or review block, not a guaranteed adaptation window.

Static image findings must never be translated into injury risk, weakness, diagnosis, or a contraindication. Symptom history, medical screening, observed exercise tolerance, and practitioner judgment govern exercise changes. Low-confidence or missing screening data should reduce personalization, not produce a more restrictive or more aggressive program.

## Evidence matrix

| Topic | Best bounded evidence | Product implication | Confidence and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistance training works; 2026 update | The new ACSM Position Stand synthesized 137 systematic reviews and more than 30,000 healthy adults. Resistance training improved strength, hypertrophy, power, endurance, gait, balance, and physical function. For strength, benefits were enhanced with loads at least 80% 1RM, complete ROM, 2-3 sets, exercises early in the session, and at least 2 sessions/week. Hypertrophy was enhanced by higher weekly volume, around 10 sets per muscle group. [Currier et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) | Make consistency and gradual progression the center of the experience. Use 2 sessions/week as the minimum plan target; offer a third session when preference and recovery allow. Do not imply that bands, bodyweight, machines, or simple routines are inferior. | High for broad adult outcomes. This is an overview of reviews, so individual prescription effects remain heterogeneous. It searched through October 2024. Verified publication timing: online 2026-03-05, issue 2026-04-01; ACSM announced it 2026-03-17. |
| Load, sets, and frequency | A Bayesian network meta-analysis of 178 strength studies and 119 hypertrophy studies found every tested prescription better than no training. Loads over 80% 1RM ranked best for maximal strength; multiset prescriptions ranked best for hypertrophy. [Currier et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/) | Start general-population users with tolerable submaximal work and progress toward heavier work only when the stated goal is maximal strength and recent logs support it. Use multiple sets after onboarding. | High for direction, lower for claiming one exact prescription is uniquely optimal. Rankings do not erase small or uncertain differences between viable programs. |
| Progressive overload: load or repetitions | In an 8-week randomized trial of 43 resistance-trained adults, progressing repetitions and progressing load produced broadly similar adaptations; load progression slightly favored dynamic strength and repetition progression modestly favored one hypertrophy site, with questionable practical differences. [Plotkin et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36199287/) | Implement double progression: earn repetitions inside a range, then add the smallest available load. A user can still progress when a machine jump is too large by adding repetitions first. | Moderate-low: one small trial, lower-body exercises, trained adults, 8 weeks. It supports flexibility, not a universal progression formula. |
| Legacy load-increase rule | The superseded 2009 ACSM stand recommended increasing load 2-10% when a trainee can perform 1-2 repetitions above the desired number. [Ratamess et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/) | A small, quantized load increase after repeated target achievement is a defensible conservative heuristic. Preserve microplate and machine-stack increments. | Consensus guidance, now superseded by the 2026 stand. The precise percentages and trigger are not validated as the single best algorithm. Label them product defaults. |
| Autoregulation | A 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis found similar strength gains from RIR/velocity autoregulation and percentage-based loading; the evidence did not show one universally superior method. [Hickmott et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35038063/) | Use recent completed sets, repetitions, RIR, pain response, and available equipment together. Do not promise RIR-based programming is superior; it is useful for adapting fixed plans to daily performance. | Moderate-low: 15 studies, mostly resistance-trained populations and varied methods. |
| RIR accuracy | A scoping review/meta-analysis of 12 studies (414 participants) found people underpredicted remaining repetitions by about 0.95 reps on average, with extreme heterogeneity; accuracy improved nearer failure and at lower repetition counts. [Halperin et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/) A study in trained men found 2-RIR estimates more accurate at 85% than at 65-75% 1RM. [Hughes et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33337690/) | Store RIR as a noisy self-report. Use bands and repeated exposures, never a single RIR value, to change load. Avoid false precision such as 0.5 RIR. | Moderate for “RIR is imperfect”; low for applying its exact error to every adult, exercise, and load. |
| Failure training | A meta-analysis of 15 studies found no significant overall difference between failure and non-failure training for strength or hypertrophy; all participants were young adults. [Grgic et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33497853/) The 2026 ACSM synthesis also found momentary fatigue/failure did not consistently affect outcomes. | Default work sets should end before failure. Reserve failure as an explicit practitioner or experienced-user choice for suitable exercises, never a required progression test. | Moderate. Definitions of failure vary, and evidence is weaker for older adults and highly trained people. Evidence does not establish exactly 2-3 RIR as uniquely optimal. |
| Periodization and undulation | A volume-equated meta-analysis of 35 studies found a small advantage for periodized training on 1RM but not hypertrophy. Undulating versus linear training favored 1RM only in trained, not untrained, subgroups. [Moesgaard et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/) The broader 2026 ACSM overview found periodization did not consistently alter outcomes. | Keep linear/double progression as the general-population default. Offer simple heavy/moderate day undulation for trained users, longer plans, or stated maximal-strength goals. Do not market undulation as necessary. | Moderate but conflicting by synthesis and training status. Differences are small and sensitive to volume matching and study design. |
| Cycle length | The 2026 ACSM review admitted trials lasting at least 6 weeks, with a 6-52 week range. The load-versus-repetition RCT observed progress over 8 weeks. Neither establishes a best 4/6/8/12-week cycle. | Treat cycle length as a commitment and reassessment cadence: 4 weeks onboarding, 6 weeks short block, 8 weeks default, 12 weeks long block with checkpoints. Continue effective plans instead of forcing novelty at the boundary. | Low for exact cycle lengths. These are product heuristics. |
| Deloads | One randomized 9-week trial of 39 trained adults found a week of complete cessation did not improve hypertrophy, endurance, or power and produced smaller lower-body strength gains than continuous training. [Coleman et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/) Delphi work notes that deloading is common but under-researched and that rigid preplanning may be unnecessary. [Bell et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/) | Do not insert a mandatory no-training week every fourth week. Offer a reduced-stress week only when logs show accumulated fatigue, persistent performance decline, poor recovery, symptoms, schedule need, or practitioner intent. Prefer reducing sets and/or effort before complete cessation. | Low. Direct trials are scarce; the cited RCT tested cessation, not every possible reduced-volume deload. Delphi consensus is expert opinion. |
| Rest intervals | A systematic review found both short and long rests can support hypertrophy, with limited evidence suggesting an advantage to rests over 60 seconds in trained participants. [Grgic et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28641044/) | Default to enough rest to repeat the planned reps and effort: 2-3 minutes for demanding multijoint work and 1-2 minutes for accessories. Let users extend rest. | Low-moderate. Exact default durations are pragmatic heuristics; available trials are few and do not justify a countdown that forces the next set. |
| Concurrent conditioning | A meta-analysis of 43 studies found concurrent aerobic plus strength training did not meaningfully reduce maximal strength or hypertrophy versus strength alone, but explosive-strength gains were attenuated; attenuation was more apparent when both modes occurred in the same session than when separated by at least 3 hours. [Schumann et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/) | Include conditioning. When explosive strength is a priority, place hard conditioning on another day or at least 3 hours away when practical. For ordinary health goals, adherence and total weekly activity take priority over perfect ordering. | Moderate. Most users are not optimizing explosive performance; subgroup findings should not become a universal prohibition. |
| Weekly aerobic and strengthening target | WHO recommends adults accumulate 150-300 min/week moderate aerobic activity, 75-150 min/week vigorous activity, or an equivalent combination, plus moderate-or-greater muscle strengthening for all major muscle groups on at least 2 days/week. [WHO, 2020](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240014886) | Show the plan’s contribution to a weekly target rather than pretending two workout sessions fulfill the entire guideline. Build conditioning gradually from the user’s baseline. | High as a public-health target, not an individualized starting dose. “Some is better than none” and gradual progression matter for inactive users. |
| e1RM and submaximal estimates | Direct 1RM tests are generally reliable, but test variation still exists (median coefficient of variation 4.2%). [Grgic et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32681399/) A 2023 IPD meta-analysis found load-velocity estimates had roughly 9.8% standard error and tended to overestimate measured 1RM; authors recommended estimates mainly for trends. [Greig et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37493929/) Repetition-to-failure equations can be exercise-specific and less accurate; a 2025 cross-validation recommended its equations only for 4-10 reps in bench press and leg extension. [Roberts et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39495260/) | Label e1RM as an estimate, keep it exercise/equipment specific, and use it for trends. Do not prescribe near-maximal loads or declare a true strength change from one estimated value. Suppress or downweight estimates from high-rep sets, uncertain RIR, technique changes, or different equipment. | Moderate for the limitations; low for generalizing any single equation across the exercise library. |
| Exercise screening and pain | ACSM’s preparticipation tool routes chest discomfort with exertion, unreasonable breathlessness, dizziness/fainting, ankle swelling, forceful/irregular heartbeat, short-distance leg cramping, or known murmur to medical clearance before starting/resuming exercise. [Exercise is Medicine/ACSM](https://www.exerciseismedicine.org/assets/page_documents/EIM%20exercise%20preparticipation%20screening.pdf) | These symptoms are stop/clearance gates, not load-adjustment inputs. New sharp, radiating, neurologic, or worsening musculoskeletal symptoms should stop the affected movement and route to practitioner review. The exact musculoskeletal rule is a conservative product safety heuristic. | High for the listed ACSM symptoms. The app must not diagnose their cause. Pain rules need clinical-owner approval; no routine practitioner override may bypass an acute stop or required clearance. Any later resumption needs its own documented clearance context. |
| Static posture and causality | An umbrella review of 41 systematic reviews found positive and null associations between posture/physical exposures and low-back pain, mixed temporality and dose response, and no consensus on causality. [Swain et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31451200/) | A scan may describe a measured position and its reliability. It must not assert weakness, pain cause, injury risk, or contraindicate an exercise. Screening uncertainty should trigger generic programming or practitioner review, not a deterministic corrective plan. | Moderate-high for rejecting causal claims; scope is spine posture/physical exposure and low-back pain, not every posture construct. |

### Additional deload evidence found during independent review

[Pancar et al., 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40612-5), published 24 February 2026, randomized limbs in 19 untrained young men to continuous training or reduced volume/frequency during weeks 4 and 8. Muscle-thickness and 10RM changes were similar between conditions. This directly concerns reduced training rather than complete cessation, but the small within-person sample, limited exercises, cross-education concerns and narrow population limit generalization. It supports deload flexibility; it does not show everyone needs a scheduled deload or validate this app's fatigue triggers.

## Selected product policies and evidence limits

The normative proposed algorithm and expected test outputs live in [PRD sections 5, 6 and 10](2026-09-07-strength-conditioning-prd.md). This annex supplies scientific support and limitations; it does not define a second progression engine.

The selected policy uses 2–3 RIR as a noisy self-report; two comparable completed exposures at the rep ceiling before a load increase; a 5% automatic increase cap with equipment-aware fractional increments; and one additional total rep assigned to the earliest below-ceiling set when effort allows. Missing RIR prevents autonomous load and rep increases. Larger changes require a separate documented coach decision and must still satisfy eligibility constraints. These are conservative testable choices, not proven optimal cut-points.

The cycle choices are 4/6/8/12-week planning horizons. Simple double progression is the adult beginner default; undulation is an option for trained users. Do not schedule automatic calendar deloads or infer capacity from a static scan. Conditioning starts at a feasible reported baseline and moves toward current activity guidance.

Do not transfer variability from direct 1RM testing into an invented meaningful-change threshold for estimated 1RM. Keep e1RM descriptive, with named equation and eligible set range, and validate its display policy before enabling it. The PRD defers any exact e1RM computation to a bounded implementation contract and keeps it out of automated load authority.

## Claims the product should reject

- “Your posture shows this muscle is weak.”
- “This alignment predicts injury.”
- “You must correct this angle before strength training.”
- “Everyone needs a deload every fourth week.”
- “Training to failure is required for growth.”
- “Undulating periodization is always better than progressive overload.”
- “Estimated 1RM is your true maximum.”
- “One difficult session proves you lost strength.”
- “Completing this in-app plan automatically satisfies WHO guidance.”

## Review requirements before implementation

Clinical ownership should approve the symptom taxonomy, pain stop language, and practitioner escalation path. Product and engineering should version the progression algorithm, retain the exact inputs and reason for every generated target, and test every evaluation case above. Any future claim that one cycle length, RIR target, deload schedule, or e1RM equation is “evidence based” should require direct evidence for that exact rule and population.
