**Engineering defaults under review:** warn = 5°, danger = 15°
**Date:** 2026-07-04

## 1. Search Summary

Five search passes were executed targeting:

1. The "Hinman 2012" n=1390 paper cited in GENU_NOTE
2. Any peer-reviewed study giving a 2D-photographic or surface-goniometric degree cut-point for frontal knee alignment that is NOT radiographic mechanical-axis/HKA
3. Normative adult surface-goniometry reference ranges for genu varum/valgum
4. Sharma 2001 JAMA for radiographic baseline context

Sources searched: PubMed/PMC (direct fetch), WebSearch with domain filtering to pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, onlinelibrary.wiley.com, jamanetwork.com, doi.org, mdpi.com.

**Critical correction identified during search:** The paper cited in GENU_NOTE as "Hinman 2012 (n=1390)" does not exist under that authorship. The n=1390 OAI paper is Riddle DL 2012, Manual Therapy — not authored by Hinman. The Hinman paper is from 2006 (Arthritis Rheum, n=40). Both are confirmed below.

## 3. Verdict

**NO CITABLE VALUE — prior CONFIRMED**

No peer-reviewed study provides a validated 2D-photographic or surface-goniometric degree cut-point for frontal knee alignment (genu varum/valgum) in adults that is independent of radiographic mechanical-axis/HKA measurement. The engineering defaults (warn=5°, danger=15°) have no citable peer-reviewed basis.

**Supporting chain:**
- Riddle DL et al. 2012, *Manual Therapy* 17(5):459-65, n=1390 (OAI): standing surface goniometry explains only ~20% of radiographic mechanical-axis variance (r≈0.50–0.54); authors explicitly conclude these measures "contain substantial error and likely do not inform clinical practice." [1]
- Hinman RS et al. 2006, *Arthritis Rheum* 55(2):306-13, n=40: surface goniometry shows NO significant correlation with mechanical axis in medial-compartment OA. [2]
- The most accurate photographic method to date (Fryzowicz 2025, n=17 accuracy cohort) requires skin-surface landmark markers, has a 3.9° systematic bias relative to radiographic HKA, and establishes no clinical cut-points. [6]
- All published degree thresholds (da Rosa 2022: ±3°; Sharma 2001: >5° functional grouping) derive exclusively from radiographic mechanical-axis measurement. [4][5]
