### Screened and excluded (wrong construct or non-transferable)

- **D'Amico et al. 2017 (PMC5480974):** 3D stereophotogrammetry; explicitly excludes upper limbs. Non-transferable.
- **Ohlendorf et al. 2023 (Sci Rep 13:873):** 3D raster stereography. Reports scapula height in mm, scapula rotation in transversal plane. Non-transferable to 2D photo.
- **Ohlendorf et al. 2022 (Sci Rep 12:6961):** Same 3D method; scapular height −0.37° is a 3D parameter. Non-transferable.
- **Akel et al. 2008 (Eur Spine J 17:348):** Photographic measure in mm only (7.5 ± 5.8 mm, n = 105 healthy adolescents); angular data (CA = 2.2 ± 1.7°, T1-tilt = 1.3 ± 1.4°) are **radiographic** (X-ray). Non-transferable.
- **Ruivo et al. 2014 (PMC4183261):** Measures sagittal-plane shoulder protraction angle (humerus midpoint–C7 vs horizontal), not frontal-plane shoulder height asymmetry. Wrong construct.
- **Braun & Amundson 1989 (PMID 2930348):** Sagittal-plane head/shoulder digitising. Wrong construct.
- **Pelvic obliquity studies (PMC10229507):** Pelvic, not shoulder. Wrong construct.
- **Menon & Pillay 2015 (PMC4650280):** 8-patient post-surgical scoliosis series; thresholds explicitly "not statistically validated." Excluded.
- **Moiré / Mrozkowiak photogrammetry studies:** Reports shoulder-line angle means but no explicit cut-point defined; moiré-based back-projection differs from 2D frontal photo landmarks. Borderline — included in candidate table as contextual reference only.

## 3. Verdict

**NO CITABLE VALUE**

No peer-reviewed study provides a formally derived, prospectively validated degree-based cut-point for frontal shoulder-line obliquity (angle of acromion line from horizontal) intended as a screening threshold for a general healthy adult population assessed from 2D standing photographs.

**Specific reasons:**

1. The only degree threshold found in the photographic shoulder-balance literature (SHA < 3° = balanced; Matamalas/Bagó 2016, Eur Spine J 25:3560) derives from the **minimal detectable change (MDC) of the measurement instrument in a scoliosis cohort** (mean Cobb 45.9°). It is a precision floor, not a normative cut-point, and the population has no bearing on healthy posture screening.

2. The only healthy-population 2D photo data (Trovato et al. 2022: 1.3–1.5° means; Penha et al. non-scoliosis FPG: 1.9° mean) provide means with SD, from which a mean + 2SD value of approximately 3.3–4.7° can be informally estimated, but **none of those authors derive or endorse a cut-point**. Publishing normative means without a validated threshold does not constitute a citable cut-point.

3. The literature reporting in mm (Akel 2008: 10 mm clinical cut-point from photographs) cannot be converted to degrees without the horizontal distance between landmarks, which is body-size-dependent and not reported. Per task criteria: non-transferable.

4. The review literature (Singla et al. 2017, J Chiropr Med 16:131) confirms that the "normal value" for the coronal shoulder angle is described qualitatively as 180° (= 0° obliquity from horizontal) but defines no deviation threshold in degrees.

**Implication for the engine:** The current engineering defaults (warn = 2°, danger = 6°) are within a defensible empirical range — the warn threshold exceeds the mean healthy-adult obliquity in every identified 2D photo study (1.3°–1.9°), and the danger threshold (6°) is near the upper end of the observed scoliosis range (Matamalas range top: 5.5° front). However, these thresholds remain engineering-derived and cannot be attributed to peer-reviewed authority.

**PROXY_NOTE status:** This metric **does not graduate** from PROXY_NOTE. The literature confirms measurability and provides descriptive means, but no reviewed paper supplies a validated cut-point for general posture screening from 2D photographs.
