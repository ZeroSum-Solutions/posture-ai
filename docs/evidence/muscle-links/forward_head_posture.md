# Literature Grading: forward_head_posture

- deep-cervical-flexors [weak]: confidence=high | citation="Khan et al. 2020 (J Chiropr Med, PMC7835487) — EMG showed asymptomatic adults with FHP had significantly reduced deep cervical flexor endurance and increased superficial muscle activation vs. normal-posture controls."
- levator-scapulae [tight]: confidence=low | citation="Janda 1988 / Kendall 2005 (textbooks) — upper-crossed syndrome classification places levator scapulae as overactive in FHP; no corroborating FHP-specific EMG or kinematic study found."
- lower-trapezius [weak]: confidence=medium | citation="Kim et al. 2015 (J Phys Ther Sci, PMID 26180310) — EMG of adults with FHP and rounded shoulders showed no significant lower trapezius difference across head positions; association is upper-crossed inference only."
- sternocleidomastoid [tight]: confidence=high | citation="Kim MS 2015 (J Phys Ther Sci, PMID 26696712) — EMG showed significantly greater SCM activation during neck rotation in adults with FHP compared to controls."
- suboccipitals [tight]: confidence=medium | citation="Lin et al. 2022 (Clin Anat, PMID 35038194) — cadaveric study showed suboccipital extensor muscles significantly shortened in FHP; direct EMG is not feasible for this muscle group with surface electrodes."
- upper-trapezius [tight]: confidence=high | citation="Khan et al. 2020 (J Chiropr Med, PMC7835487) — EMG showed significantly elevated upper trapezius activity in asymptomatic adults with FHP vs. normal posture, at rest and during arm activity."

## Notes

- **levator-scapulae**: The one PubMed EMG study of levator scapulae (PMID 8903697, Solomonow et al. 1996) examined it as a head stabilizer during arm exercises — not FHP specifically. No study directly measured levator scapulae overactivity in FHP; the role is Janda/Kendall textbook reasoning only.
- **lower-trapezius**: The best available direct-measurement study (PMID 26180310) found *no* significant lower trapezius EMG difference across head positions in FHP+rounded-shoulder adults, making this the weakest evidenced "weak" muscle in the set. Graded medium rather than low because the rounded-shoulder component of the posture has separate scapular-stabilizer literature supporting the mechanism, but this conflict should inform any screening confidence weight.
- **suboccipitals**: Surface EMG cannot adequately record suboccipitals due to their depth and muscle-spindle density; evidence rests on cadaveric length data (Lin 2022) and intervention response (PMID 27261928 — suboccipital release improved FHP), not direct activation measurement.
