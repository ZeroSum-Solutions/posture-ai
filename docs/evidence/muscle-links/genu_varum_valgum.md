# Genu Varum / Valgum — Muscle-Imbalance Evidence Grades
# Applies identically to: genu_varum_valgum_left AND genu_varum_valgum_right

- gluteus-medius [weak]: confidence=high | citation="Rinaldi et al. 2022 (J Exp Orthopaedics) — scoping review (29 studies): gluteal strength deficits consistently linked to dynamic knee valgus across EMG and kinematics studies"
- hip-adductors [tight]: confidence=low | citation="Hollman et al. 2009 (J Sport Rehab) — hip adduction angle correlated with knee valgus in step-down; no study found directly testing adductor tightness (ROM) as a cause of frontal-plane deviation"
- quadriceps [weak]: confidence=medium | citation="Park et al. 2014 (J Phys Ther Sci PMID 25435677) — genu varum showed higher VMO vs VL EMG; genu valgum showed higher VL/RF; altered patterns are a consequence and partial contributor to frontal-plane mechanics"
- tfl-it-band [tight]: confidence=medium | citation="Stickley et al. 2018 (J Athl Train) — dynamic varus measures associated with ITBS onset; TFL-ITB lateral tension mechanistically plausible for frontal-plane loading but not directly tested in a kinematics study"

## Notes
- hip-adductors [tight]: the adduction-angle/valgus correlation in Hollman 2009 is indirect — hip adduction is a kinematic outcome, not a direct test of adductor muscle tightness. No retrieved study specifically measured adductor ROM and linked it to frontal-plane knee deviation.
- quadriceps [weak]: Park 2014 describes altered VMO/VL activation patterns within alignment groups, not quad weakness as a prospective cause. Evidence is partial/indirect; the VMO-deficit hypothesis for frontal-plane mechanics remains under-powered in the retrieved literature.
- tfl-it-band [tight]: Stickley 2018 shows a varus (not valgus) association with ITBS development. The TFL-via-hip-internal-rotation pathway to knee valgus is mechanistically plausible but a direct kinematics study linking TFL tightness specifically to frontal-plane knee deviation was not found.
