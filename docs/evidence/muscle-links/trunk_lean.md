# trunk_lean — Muscle-Imbalance Evidence Grades

<!-- posture-ai literature pass 2: sagittal trunk lean (forward/backward of hips) -->

- deep-abdominals [weak]: confidence=medium | citation="PMID 27014491 (2015) — IO/TrA EMG fatigue occurred in slumped sitting in 30 office workers but not in upright or forward-lean conditions; deep core underactivity linked to forward-flexed trunk posture."
- gastrocnemius-soleus [tight]: confidence=medium | citation="Watanabe et al. 2019 (Gait Posture, PMID 30497039) — voluntary forward lean controlled by low-frequency neural input to medial gastrocnemius; calf active role in lean confirmed; tight-calf-to-lean is mechanistic inference."
- gluteus-maximus [weak]: confidence=high | citation="Ghaffari et al. 2026 (PLOS One, PMC12959714) — 8-week NASM RCT in women with LCS: glute max EMG improved significantly; gluteal inhibition confirmed as core LCS impairment alongside anterior pelvic tilt."
- hamstrings [weak]: confidence=low | citation="Janda lower-crossed / Kendall 2005 — textbook inference; hamstrings described as tight in LCS, not weak; no EMG study found linking hamstring weakness specifically to sagittal trunk lean."
- iliopsoas [tight]: confidence=high | citation="PMC11923245 (Sci Rep, 2025) — iliopsoas-tight group showed significantly altered glute max, biceps femoris, and multifidus EMG vs. normal hip-flexor group during landing (n=28 soccer players)."
- latissimus-dorsi [tight]: confidence=low | citation="Lee et al. 2016 (Ann Rehabil Med, PMC4855127) — lat activation changed with slouched sitting; no study found linking lat tightness specifically to sagittal trunk lean; Kendall 2005 textbook inference."
- lumbar-erector-spinae [tight]: confidence=high | citation="Ghaffari et al. 2026 (PLOS One, PMC12959714) — RCT: erector spinae EMG elevated in LCS women at baseline; overactivity confirmed as part of LCS pattern with anterior pelvic tilt and reduced lordosis post-intervention."
- rectus-femoris [tight]: confidence=medium | citation="Takaki et al. 2016 (Phys Ther Res, PMID 28289581) — RF among most active muscles during active anterior pelvic tilting; tight RF anteriorly tilts pelvis, contributing to compensatory trunk lean."
- thoracic-erector-spinae [tight]: confidence=medium | citation="Park et al. 2015 (PMID 25463688) — thoracic ES selective recruitment DECREASED in adults with slouched posture (inhibited, not overactive); 'tight' label contradicted by EMG; role in sway-back lean unstudied."

## Notes

- **hamstrings [weak]**: Janda's lower-crossed model primarily describes hamstrings as tight (compensating for weak glutes), not weak. Search returned PMC8393976 and PMC10538639 consistently linking hamstring extensibility to pelvic tilt as a tight/short muscle. Grade is low; consider reclassifying this role to [tight].
- **latissimus-dorsi [tight]**: Lee et al. 2016 shows lat EMG activity changes with slouching but measures activation, not length. No kinematic or stretch study links tight lats to sagittal trunk lean specifically; thoracolumbar attachment mechanism is textbook-level inference.
- **thoracic-erector-spinae [tight]**: Park et al. 2015 (PMID 25463688) is the most direct EMG study found, and it shows INHIBITION of thoracic ES in slouched posture — contradicting the "tight" label. Role in sway-back (backward trunk lean) has no study found. This association should be re-evaluated; the muscle may belong as [weak/inhibited] rather than [tight].
- **gastrocnemius-soleus [tight]**: Watanabe 2019 and Loram et al. 2005 (J Physiol, PMC1456055) confirm calves actively regulate forward lean, but the specific mechanism of tight calves causing trunk lean via limited ankle dorsiflexion is mechanistic inference not directly tested in a standing static-posture context.

## Tally
- high: 3 (gluteus-maximus, iliopsoas, lumbar-erector-spinae)
- medium: 4 (deep-abdominals, gastrocnemius-soleus, rectus-femoris, thoracic-erector-spinae)
- low: 2 (hamstrings, latissimus-dorsi)
