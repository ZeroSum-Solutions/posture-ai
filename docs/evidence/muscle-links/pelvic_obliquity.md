# pelvic_obliquity — muscle-imbalance evidence grades

- gluteus-medius [tight]: confidence=low | citation="Janda V / Kendall FP 2005 (Textbooks) — textbook inference; no EMG study found for ipsilateral glute med overactivity on elevated side."
- gluteus-medius [weak]: confidence=high | citation="Semciw A et al 2016 (J Electromyogr Kinesiol 30:98) — 13-study systematic review; reduced glute med EMG amplitude consistently associated with contralateral pelvic drop in running gait."
- hip-adductors [tight]: confidence=medium | citation="Yen W et al 2021 (Spine Deform 9:1259) — review; hip adductor contracture drives infrapelvic obliquity in people with CP; mechanistic evidence indirect, no healthy-adult kinematic study found."
- quadratus-lumborum [tight]: confidence=high | citation="Oshikawa T et al 2020 (Am J Phys Med Rehabil) — fine-wire intramuscular EMG, n=12 healthy men; anterior QL activity correlated with ipsilateral lateral pelvic elevation angle."

## Notes

- gluteus-medius opposite-role pair: weak on the dropped side (Trendelenburg mechanism, high evidence) vs inferred tight on the elevated side (low evidence; overactivity on the high side not independently measured in healthy adults in any study found).
- gluteus-medius [weak] caveat: Bahl et al 2020 (Chiropr Man Therap 28:55, PMID 33076947, n=18 healthy adults) found no significant correlation between hip abductor torque and static Trendelenburg pelvic drop. Dynamic running evidence (Semciw 2016 systematic review) and clinical gait consensus remain strong; grade holds at high.
- hip-adductors [tight]: the infrapelvic mechanism (adductor shortness → hip adduction in stance → ipsilateral pelvic elevation) is clinically coherent and demonstrated in CP populations, but no EMG or kinematic study in healthy adults directly testing this link was found — hence medium.
- quadratus-lumborum [tight]: single small study (n=12); corroborated by Janda's clinical model and QL anatomy (iliac crest attachment). High grade reflects direct in-vivo EMG evidence; confidence would upgrade further with replication.
